SSI or SSDI: which one?

SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance) is based on your work history and the Social Security taxes you paid. SSI (Supplemental Security Income) is need-based, with limits on income and resources. Some people qualify for one and some for both. The Social Security Administration decides eligibility. We help you complete the paperwork it asks for.

Common questions

Do I need a lawyer to apply for SSI or SSDI?

No. You can apply for SSI or SSDI yourself, with help from a family member or caseworker, or with help from a document preparation service. Clear Path Document Service is not a law firm. We help you understand the questions, gather what is needed and complete the forms, and we do not give legal advice or represent you before the Social Security Administration.

What is the difference between SSI and SSDI?

SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance) is based on your work history and the Social Security taxes you paid. SSI (Supplemental Security Income) is need-based and has limits on income and resources, and it does not require a work history. Some people qualify for one program, and some qualify for both. The Social Security Administration makes the eligibility decision.

How long do I have to appeal an SSI or SSDI denial?

In most cases you have 60 days from the date you receive the denial notice to ask for reconsideration, and the Social Security Administration generally assumes you received the notice 5 days after the date printed on it. Your notice explains the deadline that applies to you. If you have a denial letter, tell us the date on it as soon as you reach out so we can plan around it.
